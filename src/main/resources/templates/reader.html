Chapter 4

The Ascent

The air grew noticeably thinner as we passed the tree line. The dense pines that had shadowed our journey for the past three days gave way to sparse, stubborn shrubs clinging to the rocky soil. It was here that the true scale of the mountain revealed itself, a monolithic presence that commanded silence.

Ellas stopped to adjust his pack, his breath pluming in the crisp morning air. He did not look back at me; his gaze fixed upward. “We have about four hours of daylight left,” he muttered, more to himself than to me. The path ahead was less a trail and more a suggestion, marked only by the occasional cairn built by those who had braved this route before us.

I nodded, though he could not see it. My own legs were burning, a dull ache that had become a constant companion. Yet, amidst the physical exertion, there was a profound sense of clarity. The cacophony of the city had left behind felt like a distant dream, replaced by the rhythm of crunching gravel and steady breathing.

The stories of this place often focused on the danger—the sudden storms that could sweep in without warning, the treacherous scree slopes that threatened to send you tumbling back down. But they rarely mentioned the stillness. It was a silence so complete it felt almost heavy, a physical weight that pressed against your eardrums.

We pushed on, the incline steepening. The rhythm of our steps became a meditation. One foot in front of the other. The crunch of stone. The sharp intake of cold air. In this barren landscape, stripped of the unnecessary, only the essential remained. It was a harsh environment, yes, but in its severity, there was a strange, austere beauty.

As the afternoon wore on, shadows began to stretch across the rocky face, painting the grey stone in hues of deep violet and blue. We needed to find shelter soon. The wind had picked up, carrying with it the first faint taste of snow.

The high pass

A different kind of quiet

By dusk, we found a shallow overhang just large enough to keep the worst of the weather off our shoulders. Ellas struck a match, and for a moment the small flame made the whole mountain feel close enough to hold. We shared the last of the tea and watched the storm gather in the valley below.

“Tomorrow,” he said, “we will see the other side.”

There was no triumph in his voice. The mountain did not belong to us because we had climbed it. It had simply allowed us to pass, and the distinction mattered. I pulled my blanket tighter and listened as the first snow touched the stone like a thousand quiet fingers.

Dawn

Where the path ends

Morning arrived without ceremony. The clouds lifted slowly, revealing a valley filled with light. For the first time in days, I could see beyond the next ridge. The path was still there, narrow and pale, leading down toward a line of dark forests and a river that caught the sun.

We packed in silence. Some journeys change the destination; others change the person who arrives. As we began the descent, I understood that the mountain had offered no answers. It had offered something better: the space to hear my own questions clearly.
